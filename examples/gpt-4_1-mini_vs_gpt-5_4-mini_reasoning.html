---

- Both spoons (metal and wood) are at the same ambient room temperature, say around 22°C.
- Your hand is typically warmer than room temperature, around 33–35°C on the skin surface.

---

### Step 2: **What Does "Feeling Cold" Mean?**

- The sensation of "cold" or "warm" is related to **heat transfer** between your skin and the object.
- If heat flows **from your skin to the object**, the object feels cold.
- If heat flows **from the object to your skin**, the object feels warm.
- The **rate** of heat transfer affects how cold or warm the object feels initially.

---

### Step 3: **Thermal Conductivity of Materials**

- **Metal** has a **high thermal conductivity** (e.g., copper ~400 W/m·K, steel ~50 W/m·K).
- **Wood** has a **low thermal conductivity** (around 0.1–0.2 W/m·K).
- Thermal conductivity is a measure of how well heat can flow through a material.

---

### Step 4: **Heat Transfer at the Interface**

- When you touch the spoon, heat flows from your warmer skin to the cooler spoon.
- Because metal conducts heat well, it **quickly draws heat away** from the skin at the contact point.
- Wood, being an insulator, **draws heat away slowly**.

---

### Step 5: **Effect on Skin Temperature**

- The metal spoon rapidly removes heat from the skin, causing the skin temperature at the contact point to drop quickly.
- The wood spoon removes heat slowly, so the skin temperature drops less rapidly.

---

### Step 6: **Thermal Diffusivity and Heat Flow**

- Thermal diffusivity (thermal conductivity divided by density and specific heat) determines how fast heat spreads inside the material.
- Metal’s high thermal diffusivity means it can absorb heat from your skin and spread it throughout the spoon quickly, maintaining a cooler surface temperature at the contact point.
- Wood’s low thermal diffusivity means heat stays near the surface, warming the contact point more quickly, reducing the heat flow from your skin.

---

### Step 7: **Resulting Sensation**

- Because the metal spoon continuously draws heat away from your skin, your nerve endings sense a rapid loss of heat → **feels cold**.
- The wooden spoon draws heat away slowly, so your skin loses heat less rapidly → **feels warmer** or neutral.

---

### Summary of the Causal Chain:

1. Your skin is warmer than the spoon.
2. Touching the spoon causes heat to flow from skin to spoon.
3. Metal’s high thermal conductivity allows rapid heat flow away from skin.
4. Rapid heat loss cools the skin at the contact point quickly.
5. The cooling of skin triggers nerve signals interpreted as "cold."
6. Wood’s low thermal conductivity slows heat flow.
7. Skin cools slowly, so the sensation of cold is less pronounced.
